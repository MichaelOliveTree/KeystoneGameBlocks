THESIS:
-----------------------------------------------------
Games can be made much more interesting if NPCs (Non-Player Characters) behaved in more "interesting" ways.  Games with more interesting NPCs will DRAMATICALLY INCREASE the global audience of interactive digital entertainment.  "Interesting" can be measured or tested by determining
	a) how long the average USER(S) will [want to] watch or observe a single NPC's BEHAVIOR in a simulation, without interacting directly with that NPC in any way AND
	b) where that NPC is restricted to a SINGLE SCENE or ENVIORNMENT [the EXHIBIT] that is no greater than 10x10 meters or 100 sqare meters (~1076 square feet for the Yanks).  

MISSION:
-----------------------------------------------------
NPC Behaviors in modern games are so EMBARASSINGLY and ATTROCIOUSLY bad, the Games Industry needs an INTERVENTION.  It simply does not seem to care much about advancing the quality of NPCs within their simulations.  Have we really come that much farther than the days of "Creatures", "Daikatana", the dog in "Fable", nVidia's 2002 "Dawn" demo and "The Sims?"  AI capabilities in games seem like they are always the first to get cut/postponed.  The industry seems to CONSTANTLY shut down/scale back the implementation of enhanced AI for NPCs in upcoming games due to "resource and time constraints."

EXHIBIT (aka "Scene") (no, not the Demo Scene  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e-qjLfnOrx8  )
-----------------------------------------------------
This FISHBOWL CHALLENGE can be as deep/involved/detailed/difficult as one dares to make it.  This is the whole point!

This challenge should be of a TECHNICAL nature and not of an artistic or aesthetic one.  We are NOT trying to dazzle the USER with visuals, we are trying to dazzle them with NPC BEHAVIOR.  With this in mind, the Exhibits should all be very similar so that the various submissions can be evaluated\compared fairly.

With this in mind, for "Exhibit A" I propose a house on the order of 10x10 meters similar to the one depicted in the film "Up" and inhabited by a single NPC character.



EVALUATING THE SUBMISSIONS
-----------------------------------------------------
 
Determine the amount of run-time  memory, disk storage and processing power is needed to reach the various thresholds.
	a) how is this impacted over time by NPCs that can "memorize" and "learn" on-the-fly? 
		i- how important is on-the-fly learning and knowledge accumulation for creating an engaging EXHIBIT?
 

- Memory Use
- CPU / GPU Performance
- Size of code / libraries
- Simplicity/Elegance of the code
- Simplicity/Elegance of the API
- Extensibility of the code and the NPC Behaviors
- Scaleability

- [Un]Predictability of the  NPC  (moving away from the dreaded ARCHETYPES (2D cardboard caricatures) because we can't create fully fledged characters very easily/cheaply)
- NPC Learning (Memorizing new information and ways of achieving goals)
- # Simultaneous Behaviors that an NPC can run
- Existance of Emergent Behaviors 
	- I believe that emergent behaviors are more likely to arrise from simple/elegant systems (or systems with simple \ elegant rules) that can be combined in various ways to create more complex behaviors.
- What behaviors are being simulated?
	- Autotomic(and similar) functions (eg. breathing, eye-blinking, hiccups, sneazing, yawning)
	- Appropriate responses to stimulii (answering a ringing phone and engaging in interesting coversations with daughter, salesman, robocaller, wrong number, etc)
        - Emotional Range - emotions of various kinds performed in appropriate situations
