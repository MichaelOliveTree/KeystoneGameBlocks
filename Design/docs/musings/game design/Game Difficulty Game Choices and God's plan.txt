God does what God does and that may or may not work out well for you.  However, every person is tasked with being the best they can be given what they have.  How many can say that they have not at least in part, squandered the opportunities they had because they felt like they were not given as good an opportunity as their brother or sister?

Just because you were born to crappy parents, in a crappy neighborhood, with crappy schools doesn't mean you are not meant to try to be the best you can be.  

God's plan for you is spelled out in the Word itself... the physical world, the physical body you've been given, your mind, and your spirit for life, and not just for surviving.

If you are the best you can be, then you will not regret your choices and you will not morn your own misfortune... because it is God's plan... and His plan will result in some manner of suffering for all at some point for we are all tested and if we fail that test, we will individually CHOOSE to walk through the gates of hell instead of heaven.  
  
What does being the best you can be mean?  It means following the natural tendancies of your species to explore, seek truth, assist one another, and do all of these things in harmony and in so doing... reveal ourselves to be one with each other and thus with God.

Robot Caesar  - The Gates (Failing Tests leads to Torment - but we will walk through the gates of torment by choice, not by gunpoint.  That is why failing means you must get up and re-take the test and never stop trying.)

- This is a philosophy of game design even... and why the metaphor of retaking levels you've failed until you get it right is so rewarding at the end.

- It is why designing games to be addictive is wrong... making games for the lowest common denominator is wrong.  

- Choices in games should reflect the choices we make when it comes to being tested, failing a test, and then deciding whether we want to get up, retake the test until we pass, or cheat, or give up, or 

In Robot Caesar, an "angel" of some kind, may challenge Caesar to get up, try again... a devil might urge him to quit, and to curse the gods for his misfortune... to try and easier level instead...  Then he will present a very attractive looking gate, one that seems to solve all the problems, is seductive and attractive and looks better than the previous... and if the player enters it, after having freely chosen to do so, they will find that they sacrifice peace, health (chemicals are offered and chemical dependance occurs), confidence, dignity, certainty for confusion, and a dream that becomes a nightmare.

Different types of angels appear in all forms... the signs are there, and the player has to decide from their heart, what do they mean... what are they telling them.  In a way, these signs come from their own inner selves... the significance they place on them comes from within suggesting we already know what we should do, but what do we actually choose and when tested heavily... do we break down... do we wonder what would have happened if they failed just one more time?

- Doom 2 reminds me a lot of an action roguelike where the player has already chosen the worst possible choices and is now in a truly  nightmarish place.  But part of what it lacked was a sense of being really a place and not just a series of game maps... and that is where it moved away from Simulation and into Aracde.  

This is especially true in light of the other games that were out there... games that had been creating open worlds even if they required lots of loading between areas... but you could always go back to a previous area.  In Wolfenstein and others, the "LEVEL" became a big thing in games and it became a way to tailor gameplay and pacing and such.  

- what if we could re-create a parody of a Doom 2 level... especially that first opening level everyone has played a gazillion times... and then... instead of leaving the "level", they exit out of this cavern and into a truely open world.
