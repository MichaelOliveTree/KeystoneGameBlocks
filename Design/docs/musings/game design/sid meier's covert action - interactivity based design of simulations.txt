nov.19.2014 - Sid Meier's Covert Action <-- interactivity based design of simulations (interface based)

http://www.rockpapershotgun.com/2014/11/18/covert-action-review/

when you look at an early game like Sid Meier's Covert Action you see a design that 

1) sets out to simulate a fantasy job like "spy"
2) defines the types of missions you can do
3) creates an interface for those missions
	- bare minimum abstract design to create the sense and provide relevant information
4) ties them all together in an overarching agent & task based simulation of the world 


Other games that follows this principle are Krellan Commander, XCOM (with it's GeoView, BaseViews, MissionViews), Zelda (with the different views it has of overworld, dungeons, shops), Dwarf Fortress, etc.

In a sense, every part of the interface is both game-y, as well as complimenting/supporting/fullfilling the overall games design goals.

When Sid Meier's said "make just one game" he was referring to this... that the many sub-games exist to support the main game.  They don't exist to be their own seperate games.  That is a great example of how Spore went wrong.



Games that go too far in this regard are games like Rules of Engagement.
