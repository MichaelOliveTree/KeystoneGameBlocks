i was thinking as i was carrying groceries into my apartment today (11.17.2007) that when i was opening the main door to my building, i stuck my foot back to stop it from slamming but it never did and i instantnly realized that it was because of the resistance from the carpeting that wasnt there before and when the door didnt slam back against my foot (which i had early anticipated to stop the slam) in the time i expected i instantly realized that it was slwoly going to shut due to the floor mat.   And then it occurred to me... wow, how is it a human brain can perform so much processing in such a quick time.... i mean afterall, this all occurred between the time i opened the door and was waiting it to slam against my foot but then realized it wouldnt and it was ok to walk away. IMAGINE THIS!  It struck me for some reason taht our brains must be able to sort of "push" to the foreground a relevant set of experiences to use to "solve" for a problem and that is what it does best.  Then i thought, this explains the process of being "startled"  it's what happens when something occurs that is not in that relevant set to solve tha tproblem... your brain suddenly has to "switch gears" to deal with something it wasnt "mapped" at the moment to deal with.   a sort of "cache miss" to use a computer cpu analogy
