March.19.2015  - The Bourne Identity (novel by Robert Ludlum - google other books by him to see if there's a similar pattern)

All good films are layered and the Bourne Identity is no exception.

The fundamental quality of the film that makes it so relateable is REGRET and the desire to change one's path in life... to undo that decision that sent them down a path that turned them into a person they cant stand to look at in the mirror and to become somebody else.

Bourne is an example of someone who is litterally fighting to become someone else.  To become free.  

The amnesia he suffers is more than a plot device... it symbolizes Bournes subconscious desire to be somebody else.  To forget that he is a monster and to choose again... this time to be his own man... and not a government assassin.

The "secret agent" persona is a great role to use for this purpose because unlike 007, real life assassins probably really do hate themselves.  I think we see that with Man on Fire which is a story about redemption as well as regret and self loathing.  

I imagine that with Bourne, the writer started by watching some spy flick or assassin flick and wondered what if he could take a spy who regrets his decision to become what he is, and give him a second chance... would he make the same decision twice?

The amnesia affords us that chance at making the decision without the foreknowledge of what he would become.

I think considering the above, this method of writing a new story, is not quite like the reverse engineering method - it takes a short cut of taking an existing genre and telling a familiar, but fundamentally different story based on something deeper than the obstensibly simplistic plots we normally see.
