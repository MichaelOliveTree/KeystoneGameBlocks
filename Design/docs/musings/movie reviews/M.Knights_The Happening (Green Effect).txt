I read in a couple of hours and then wrote a review/critique of the M.Knight screenplay for "The Happening."  I'll be very curious to see what if anything get's changed in the movie and how right/wrong I might've been about anything.

I just read the entire thing and I know its not the final script but, I'm skeptical that this movie as is can be pulled off by anyone. I wrote this up quickly so forgive the spelling errors and grammar. Im tired and dont want to spend alot of time on this.

========================================================================
The Good

- The initial suspense is good and get's going pretty fast. I think moreso than any of his other films, the suspense starts and is sustained for far a long time.

- The visuals for the panic and hysteria in Philly are good.

- Some specific scenes also really stand out such as when Julian conquers his fear and decides to go look for his wife.


The Bad

- The subject matter itself. Nobody is going to buy into this story as is. My initial reaction when reaching page 32/33 was that either M. Knight has lost his mind completely or that this script is a hoax. I wouldn't think he could find anyone to even fund this production.

- There's not enough confusion early on about what's causing the attack. At least from the script, I never thought for a second that this was a terrorist attack or anything else. That you can guess so easily that this was some type of airborne spore or fungus diminishes the impact of the full reveal on 32/33.

- I would've liked it more had there been some question THE ENTIRE TIME leading up to the end, as to whether this was the Rapture, a terrorist strike, a plague/bacteria/virus, alien parasite/body snatchers, an Illuminati conspiracy to kill off the population, etc. For instance, he could have put some red herrings in there (e.g jet's laying down chemtrails, U.S launching military strikes against cargo / container ships coming to port in Seattle because of fears of dirty bombs. News articles about the Illuminati sitting in teh background, movie posters for fictious scifi NanoBots Attack, etc) to confuse us into what was going on. Thus you have a general disaster movie, mixed with horror and not knowing who the enemy is, and just a gazillion questions errupting in the audiences minds with everyone confused (but in a good way.) Of course this could mean changing the nature of how some people died. And the ANIMALS NOT DYING is a dead give away too.

- Julian seemed to be a more noble character than the Elliot character and I'm not sure that's such a good thing.

- Repetitive. From about page 32/33 when the key reveal is made up to page 82 it's just classic (i.e. cliche) horror where people/groups are getting picked off one by one. This woulnd't be so bad _IF_ there was still confusion about what was causing the deaths.

- Plot Holes and Illogical Behavior. Why not seek out gas masks in the short term? I guarantee alot of people will find that not doing this defies all reason. The selective triggering and killing is going to be very difficult for people to swallow as well.

- Despite the explanations they gave each other, I just don't fully understand the anger between those two. I mean he sounds a bit like a hippie (I can picture a younger version of Jeff Bridges playing that character) and she seems similar except she's got one foot stepping into the practical world where financial success and material persuits can make for a more comfortable life.

- The killing of the "angry" Christian women at the end. It seemed like an unnecessary stretch to say "She's killed because she's angry and withdrawn from the world and has replaced it with relgion." I mean she lived a life out in the woods, off the grid, closer to in harmony with nature than any of the main surviving characters... seemed like she was just killed for being Christian and I dont think it works in the film or that its the right type of message to be sending.



Suggestions

- Maybe add that there's been some unsual weather patterns leading up to this date/time. These weather patterns triggered a certain type of prehistoric plant/fungus/mold/mushroom/etc that has been around since the dawn of man is now germinating everywhere. Global warming could be a reason why even in some climates this plant/mushroom/fungus/etc has come back. Human activity raises the temperature, and now nature has a built in solution to the human problem. (but nothing so screwball as everyday plants releasing and selectively targeting toxins at people).

See this story for a recent fungus outbreak near where I live...
http://seattletimes.nwsource.com/html/localnews/2003657226_canfungus08.html

- If it was just a certain type of plant/fungus it'd make the concept somewhat more believable. For instance, maybe like the 7 year cicada insect, there's some really ancient plant that is now out of some insanely long (e.g. 10,000 year) dormancy, is growing and is very primitive and as a defense mechanism, release a chemical from an air bladder / microscopic bubble/pockets that line the surface of its leaves or whatever and releases a poison in a very short radius (and the poison is neutralized by the air/wind very quickly so it never lasts long) and this neurotoxin can have various effects on peoples neurological system. Some people might become extremely euphorious and think they can fly. Some will become suicidal. Some will become murderous and schitzo, etc. But I dont think everyone should strictly kill themselves and such.

- I dont think M.Night would like the above suggestion because it makes the whole thing less ethereal. In his script, the plants have a sort of consciousness (metaphysical mumbo jumbo) and I doubt he'd want to try and make it more scientific.

- Remove the reveal from 32/33. Get rid of the entire concept of him having taught a class on this phenomenon. Frankly I think it'd be a better movie if we did not have _ANY_ clear rationale explained for what was going on... that it'd be better if they were constantly left to guess the entire time and perhaps finding "clues" of some kind that helped them become survivors (sort of like a helping hand... that they were destined to be left in the new Eden).

- In his science class, perhaps instead of them watching the slide show about cave paintings, how about something about the extinction of the dinosaurs, how some dinosaurs did survive and that today they have evolved into birds. Then he can introduce theories abotu why the dinosaurs (or some other mass extinction in the earth's history) took place? Some kids will talk about meteors, some about the weather changing, the atmsophere getting thinner, and he can introduce a more vague concept "That they had become too dominant and that nature just found a way to deal with the dinosaur problem." Nature always wins. Nature always finds balance. (yes this is cliche thing to do in movies, but i dont thnk its so bad in this case)

- Keep more of the people who got off the train into the town together, and have some fights\arguments\etc between passengers start up
- Family A not wanting to be near Family B because they think Family B is infected because they came from some different part of town that god killed off earlier.
- A Muslim family is harrassed because some people think its Islamic Terrorists.
- A Jewish family is blamed by some extremist Christians that God is coming to cleanse the world... that it's the Rapture or something.
- A wierd looking nut job telling everyone stories about how the world is run by Lizard Men and they are finally exterminating 2/3 of the world population as planned (could be funny).
- In short, I think (at least because the whole relationship issue between Alma and Elliot is so inconsequential given the crisis at hand) that there needs to be other conflicts to help really give a feeling of hysteria and to keep the audience off balance.

- I sort of have a mental picture of Logan's Run only on the surface of the planet taking place. I don't know why this appeals to me for this type of movie... probably because once he got to the surface everything was just like a garden of eden. I think in The Happening the characters are of course "running". In Logan's Run they were looking for "sactuary." But its kind of wierd that the "sanctuary" in THe Happening is found in their hearts... i mean if that's the message its not PAINTED very well... instead it's just "revealed" in alot of dialogue by Elliot... and that sort of thing is a good sign that your story has problems.  It's the human elements to the story people are going to find compelling and not the metaphysical plant consciousness vengeance/payback and balance spiel.  



Anyway, I'm not comfortable with the script as it is, but then who the hell am I? heheh. I've never written a screenplay or finished any story since college many moon ago.
