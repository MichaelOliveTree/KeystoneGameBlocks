Let's list off his accomplishments

- live in an old folks home
- plays the piano
- has sex in a brothel
- gets drunk many times
- works on a tugboat
- has an affair with a married woman
- survives a u-boat's machine gun attack at sea
- inherits a large estate and business
- learns to sail
- lives life like a millionaire playboy
- get's married
- has a kid
- leaves all his wealth to his wife
- abandons wife and kid
- does the whole "Free Tibet!" world touring hippie thing
- dies in his wife's arms.

So here we have a man who for the most part, held one job working on a tugboat before he became filthy rich and then did nothing for anyone except himself, and later his wife and kid. And let's face it, sailors who work on tug boats or fishing boats are the type of people who could never hold "real" jobs on land unless it was something like taxi driving. Sure it's all nice and good to live a mostly care free existance, but the other 99% of us have responsibilities and goals and dreams. Figures that Benjamin would become a bum once he gave up all his wealth.

So, besides the people who might recall him because of his curious condition, who besides his daughter will ever really remember Benjamin Button?

Nobody.

I realize he was developmentally stunted in many ways. Not having a real childhood is bound to screw you up inside to a certain degree. But still, Benjamin Button was virtually provided for his entire life. Great performances aside, what's to like or appreciate about Benjamin Button the movie or the person? When I look at Benjamin Button I see a squandered life brimming with mediocrity. He didn't excel in anything or try to become anything.

We have enough mediocre people in this world.

Well if you pretend that the Benjamin Button universe continues then I'm sure you can say that someone somewhere out there will remember him.  

But based on the film I saw, he did nothing of note that anyone we were introduced to, who were alive or assumed to be alive as the credits rolled, would remember.

So you can imagine that maybe he did all these great things while touring the world after he abandoned his wife and kid, but it's pure speculation.  There's no reason to believe he was any less care free and void of responsibility then as he was before.

In fact, the arrival of the kid and the responsibility it would bring is probably the reason he took off.  This is probably the best rationale for his leaving.  Benjamin Button was not this "wise old man" in a young man's body who carefully determined that the best interest of his child and wife was to abandon them.  This was a guy who never grew up and when he found out his wife was pregnant became terrified at what that child represented.

Maybe after growing up in an old folks home stuck with a bunch of old people that looked like him and who were dropping like flies, the idea of taking roots, becoming stagnant and living out your days waiting to die was something he refused to have any part of.  I can dig that and I can dig the irony that at the end he ends up dying in that old folks home anyway.  But it doesnt change the fact that in my opinion, he didn't seem to give as much to the world as the world had given him.  He was completely mediocre.
