Disproportionality in Killing by U.S in War 
(posted in reply to rebuttals to earlier comments i made on pvponline forumn)

I'll admit the point I was trying to make are a bit convoluted so i'll reitterate and clarify.

- I feel there's an inherent hypocracy built into the notion that some weapons or their use are legitimate and others not.  I'm not saying all weapons are either legitimate or illegitmate just that the classification itself is inappropriate.  So the discussion of whether land minds can be "ok" to use on the basis of perceived legitimacy of the devices or their potential uses (i.e. that they can alternately be used in legitimate way and illegitimate way) is not (in my opinion) a valid way for advocating their use one way or the other.

- On the other hand, criticising landmines on the basis of their effectiveness as a strategic or tactical weapon is a valid one.  I for one don't think they are effective and there have been studies which show this.  "More and more military leaders are admitting that landmines are simply not militarily decisive. In 1995, the International Committee of the Red Cross and the Red Crescent commissioned a military study of the effectiveness of landmines. Examining 26 conflicts since 1940, the study found that anti-personnel mines played no significant role in the outcome of any of them."  But this is beside the point I'm trying to make.

- The primary criticism of landminds is that they kill indiscriminantly.  They do not strictly target combatants exclusively.  

- The indiscriminant attacking nature of landminds is central to the (inappropriate imo) argument on the legitimacy of landminds as a weapon.   

- When one starts talking about legitimate uses of weapons in war the hypocracy that I sense comes from the inference that it depends on how the weapon is used which invariably leads to "who" is employing these weapons and all too often these perceptions aren't based on any objective criteria.  They are biased depending on which side you are on or who's propoganda you buy into. 

- I then proceeded to use the United States as an example of this hypocracy not out of bias, but precicely because of the broad perception (at least in the West) that U.S weapons and their employment in war is somehow a shining example of "legitimate," "clean" or dare I say "good" use when in fact, we kill and have killed directly and indirectly, indiscriminantly and disproprortionately so, in many wars and conflicts.

	- In Japan we killed ~600,000 civilians during the fire bombing campaigns and the nuking of Hiroshima and Nagasaki.
	- In Iraq we've used cluster bomb munitions.  
	- In Iraq we've used white phosphorous 
	- In Iraq we use DU munitions which result in all sorts of health problems for people exposed (soldiers and civilians alike).
	- ~600,000 Iraqi's killed.  I'd like to see where this has been "thoroughly debunked." The number may be too high or too low but undisputed is the disproportionality of the carnage. I'm not talking about U.S casualties in the war compared to Iraqi casualties.  This being a pre-emptive war I suppose its a question of how many Iraqi civilians killed is ok in our "war on terror."  More loaded phrases.
	- Start adding up people with missing limbs and other major injuries and the number of lives ruined doubles or more.
	- In vietnam we've killed high hundreds of thousands if not millions of civilians during carpet bombing campaigns... especially during Operation Rolling Thunder where ~1million sorties were flown dropping 750 million tons of dumb bombs on the North.  The stats Ive found on line vary quite a bit.  The low end for total casualties is 1.5, the average by number of sites reporting the figure is 2.5 million and there are some that report 1million combatants killed and 4 million civilians.  Crazy numbers.  This compared to ~58k U.S casualties.  But make no mistake about it, most of the deaths were do to the aerial bombing and not as a result of classes between South and North vietnamese combatants.
	- In Vietnam estimates of ~300 people a year are still killed from unexploded cluster munitions left over from the Vietnam War.

So you see, there's no reversal here.  When you talk about legitimate\illegitimate weapons this leads to discussions on legitimate\illegitimate targets and legitimate\illegitimate killings.  Soon you find that there really are no such things in war.  I think for all sides concerned there is only the outcome that matters no matter which side one is on.  War is the poster boy for the phrase "the ends justifying the means."  I'll admit that this has all been a bit much for me to launch into based on the use of a single word.  In this case I found the characterization not only inappropriate but I just found it to be a loaded word in that context.

The extra bit about Japan I added I should've left out. I didn't want to imply that "they deserved" what they got or anything like that.  Intentional killing of civilians by your enemy doesnt give you a moral license to intentionally kill theirs.  My intent was only to show that it wasn't just the U.S indiscriminantly killing in the Pacific Theater.  I think you're right about Japan's reasons for attacking Pearl Harbor.  We had an embargo on oil and scrap metal to Japan and if they could destroy our Pacific Fleet, they'd be largely unopposed in taking over islands in the Pacific to get access to more resources.
