My reply:

Words can be powerful. Not trying to single CVGurau out because I know alot of people use the word "tolerate" and all of its derivatives to describe how people should relate to other people who are different. But I for one have grown to hate that word. Think of the things one would tolerate. A cold, allergies, your dog peeing on the rug because you were 2 hours late getting home, an election result that didn't go in your favor, your best friend's bad breath, being a prisoner of war, etc. These are things where if you could, you would wish them all away forever. It's a rather schitzophrenic word (or at least how we tend to use it) because there is a mostly false sense of having the power to "not tolerate" something. "I'm a superior human being because I choose to tolerate your pathetic existance. Ha Ha Ha!" In reality, most things we end up tolerating we either have no control over or the alternative is even more unacceptable. Because we are unable to eradicate certain things, we must tolerate them.

To advocate tolerance of gays, or blacks or jews or homeless people or ugly people, is to subtly suggest to people "Look, I understand these are some vile creatures but do me a favor and resist the temptation of forming a lynch mob and stringing them up. Ok?" Less dramatic and practically speaking, an employer who merely tolerates women in the upper echelons of a corporation to meet some quota or to achieve an outward appearance of being progressive is not going to fairly promote and compensate women in those positions.

Politicians love to tell people all the things they should just tolerate because it more or less just says do nothing about it. Tolerance is why you'll let a gay person sit next to you on the bus one day and vote against gay marriage at the polls the next. Tolerance is why you'll play racketball with a black co-worker to get a work out, but God forbid your daughter should actually marry one.

I know none of what I'm saying is revelation. We all already know these things (when we're thinking critically and not wandering around on zombie autopilot) but I think sometimes people forget and they use the word tolerate when what they really mean to say is "embrace." Embrace diversity. Embrace different cultures and ways of life. Embrace new ideas and viewpoints. Embrace change.
