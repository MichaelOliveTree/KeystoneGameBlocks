april.1.2015 - playwrites vs writers of novels

there's something about writing a play I think - including the process of performing the play and revising it based on the audience reactions - that is much closer to game development than film making.

playwriting winds up being inherently collaborative where actors, costumers, set builders, and even the audience all incrementally improve upon the play and get it closer to perfection.

There was a scene in "Night of the Iguana" with Richard Burton and Eva Gardner and Deborah Kerr where Eva's character Maxine is listening in the conversation of the other two and when Kerr's character mentions "Maxine", Maxine springs out suddenly and then we all know she'd been listening all along.  

That scene if originally written by a screenwriter would have skipped back and forth between shots of Maxine eavesdropping and of the other two during their intense, private conversation - but fortunately we didn't need that and we knew Maxine had been there all along even if perhaps the other two didn't pick up on it.  

that was superior to cutting back and forth between two shots.
