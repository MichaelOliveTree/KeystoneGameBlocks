Larry makes a good point. In fact, intra school competition (teams or individual) probably detracts from instilling physical health consciousness across a wide variety of people because it shifts the attention away from well being to "achievement."

And treating physical education like a chore that one should take part in for a scheduled period each day is not a good way to instill health consciousness either. It may fit into a certain western worker life style from employers perspective who don't want to facilitate a healthy workforce on their own dime, but it doesn't lead to a healthy society.

Physical health relates to mental health and when mental health is challenged (family issues, societal pressures, financial pressures, cultural messages), physical health gets challenged too. So much of the thinking around dealing with socio-economic problems (eg. growing obesity epidemics) start with acceptance of certain worker-centric realities and ultra liberal capitalistic philosophies (every man for himself individualism) that must be worked around because those realities are considered immutable. So we're not much interested in solving problems as we are in mitigating them within a set context (eg. shifting all responsibility to the individual). That is doomed to failure because it rules out (strips away support for) the type of education that would undermine that reality.
